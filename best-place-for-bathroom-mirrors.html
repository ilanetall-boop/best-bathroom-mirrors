Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Center the mirror on your sink, not your vanity. If the vanity is off-center in the room, follow the sink, not the countertop edges, or the mirror looks crooked even when it's perfectly level.
  • Leave 2 to 4 inches of clearance on each side. A mirror that runs edge to edge with your vanity looks stretched, and one with more than 6 inches of bare wall on either side looks undersized.
  • Anti-fog glass only works while it's plugged in and switched on. Every anti-fog panel in this comparison needs a nearby outlet, and the heating element takes a few minutes to clear the glass after you turn it on.
  • Match mirror width to sink count. A single 24 to 32 inch mirror suits one sink; double vanities need either two mirrors or one continuous mirror in the 48 to 60 inch range, like the GarveeHome in this guide.
  • LED color temperature affects how you look, not only how well you can see. Mirrors in the 3000K to 4000K range read as warm to neutral light, closer to how you'll look under most home lighting than a bluish, daylight-toned setting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best place for bathroom mirrors?

The best place for bathroom mirrors is centered above the sink rather than the vanity's outer edges, with the bottom of the mirror five to ten inches above the faucet and the middle of the glass at eye level for whoever uses that sink most. On a double vanity, center the mirror or mirrors over each sink individually rather than centering one mirror on the whole countertop.

How high should a bathroom mirror be mounted?

Most bathroom mirrors are mounted with the bottom edge 5 to 10 inches above the faucet, which typically puts the center of the glass close to eye level for an adult of average height. If more than one person of noticeably different height shares the vanity, a taller mirror like the 36 inch MYSUNORIA or Ratsamee in this comparison gives more flexibility than a shorter mirror mounted higher on the wall.

Should a bathroom mirror be wider than the vanity?

No. A mirror wider than the vanity below it usually looks unbalanced and can crowd nearby light fixtures, outlets or a medicine cabinet. Sizing the mirror 2 to 4 inches narrower than the vanity on each side, the pattern behind every mirror in this comparison, keeps the wall looking proportioned while still leaving enough reflective surface for daily use.
